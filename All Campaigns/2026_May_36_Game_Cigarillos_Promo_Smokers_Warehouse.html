Methodology

1 — Redemption Rate

Pull loyalty transactions containing any Game Cigarillo SKU where 3+ units appear in the basket. Divide by total loyalty cigarillo baskets in the period. A high redemption rate means the threshold is easy to reach; a low rate means most buyers aren’t hitting the minimum purchase quantity.

2 — Flavor Mix

For qualifying loyalty baskets (3+ units), which flavor combinations are most popular? Single-flavor bulk buying vs. multi-flavor mixing-and-matching tells you whether customers are stocking a favorite or experimenting. Multi-flavor combos validate the “any 3 flavors” mechanic; single-flavor dominance suggests the deal is mostly a bulk price.

3 — Cost of Promotion

Discount per set = (regular retail × 3) − $2.75 = $1.12 per set. Total promo cost = total loyalty sets redeemed × $1.12. This is the gross discount given to loyalty customers. Compare to the regular-price value of those units to compute the effective discount rate.

4 — Loyalty Focus

Cross-reference redeemers against loyalty history. Existing Game buyers = cards seen buying Game before the campaign month (loyalty reinforcement — acceptable). New Game buyers = first-time Game purchasers. New tobacco = never bought any tobacco before. Compare redeemers’ visit frequency in the campaign month vs. the prior month — higher frequency signals genuine engagement; same frequency + higher units signals pantry-loading.

5 — Incremental Volume

Did loyalty cigarillo unit volume in the campaign month exceed the 3-month trailing average? A spike (>30% above average) with flat visit frequency = pantry-loading risk. Pantry-loading means customers stocked up and may reduce purchases in the following month — flag this for June monitoring.
